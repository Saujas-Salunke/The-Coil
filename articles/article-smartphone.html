New Smartphone Feature Lets You Ignore Notifications In Even More Efficient Ways

The latest software update promises to help users avoid their messages faster than ever, with a redesigned "definitely later" button.

By Renata Dubois Published September 16, 2026 Updated September 16, 2026 Reading time 4 min

The Coil is a fictional parody publication. All stories and quotes are created for demonstration purposes.

A major smartphone manufacturer released a software update this week featuring what it calls "the most intuitive way yet" to avoid dealing with your notifications. The headline addition, a redesigned "Snooze All" button, allows users to dismiss an entire notification tray in a single tap, without the inconvenience of reading a single one of them first.

"We looked at how people actually use notifications," said a product spokesperson during a press briefing, "and we found that most people don't read them so much as they acknowledge their existence and then continue with their day. We wanted to make that process faster."

Efficiency, redefined

According to internal testing, the update reduces the average time between "notification received" and "notification dismissed without reading" from 4.2 seconds to 1.8 seconds — a company record. Beta testers reportedly described the change as "life-changing," though several later admitted they weren't sure what any of the dismissed notifications had actually said.

"I've dismissed 41 notifications today. I have no idea what any of them were about. This is the fastest I've ever felt," said one beta tester.

The update also introduces a new "Quiet Confidence" mode, which displays a small checkmark next to the notification bell to suggest that everything has been handled, regardless of whether it has.

Not everyone is thrilled

Not all reactions have been positive. Some users have raised concerns that the feature makes it too easy to miss genuinely important alerts, such as calendar reminders, security codes, or messages from people who are, in fact, waiting for a reply.

"I dismissed a notification about my own dentist appointment," said one user in an online forum. "I found out it happened when I got a call asking where I was. In my defense, the button was right there."

The company's response

In response to the criticism, the manufacturer noted that users can still open individual notifications manually if they "really want to know what's going on," though the company acknowledged this workflow is "significantly less popular" than simply dismissing everything at once.

The update is rolling out gradually over the next two weeks. Early data suggests most users will have it enabled — and most notifications dismissed unread — well before that rollout finishes.
