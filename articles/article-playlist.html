Researchers Discover The Perfect Study Playlist Contains Exactly 0 Minutes Of Studying

A groundbreaking new study reveals students retain more information when listening to songs about literally anything other than schoolwork.

By Renata Dubois Published September 19, 2026 Updated September 19, 2026 Reading time 4 min

The Coil is a fictional parody publication. All stories and quotes are created for demonstration purposes.

In a stunning development that has baffled both educators and streaming service curators, researchers have determined that the most effective study playlists contain absolutely zero references to studying, learning, or academic achievement. The findings, published in the Journal of Obvious Outcomes, suggest that songs titled "Crunch Time," "Study Session Beats," and "All Nighter" actually reduce retention by 73 percent.

"We were looking at hundreds of 'focus' playlists," said lead researcher Dr. James Martinez. "And we kept seeing songs about homework, exams, and pulling all-nighters. Then we realized—the problem isn't the tempo or the genre. It's that the lyrics remind students they should be working instead of working." The team concluded that perfect study music includes songs about parties, driving cars, falling in love, and things that have nothing to do with books.

The counter-intuitive conclusion

According to the study, students exposed to tracks like "Dancing Queen" or "Hotel California" scored 42 percent higher on subsequent quizzes than those listening to lo-fi beats explicitly marketed as "Study Vibes Vol. 7."

"I was using a playlist called 'Finals Week Motivation' for three semesters. Turns out hearing someone rap about staying up late studying made me more stressed. Now I listen to Taylor Swift and somehow pass my chemistry exam. Science," posted one graduate student.

Music streaming platforms responded cautiously. Spotify declined to remove existing study-themed playlists but added a disclaimer: "Songs about studying may cause ironic effects related to studying." Apple Music introduced a new feature that auto-filters any track mentioning the word "homework."

Students react

Undergraduate populations took the news with characteristic enthusiasm mixed with confusion. Several admitted to having deleted playlists simply because they sounded too productive.

"I had a playlist named 'Get That Degree' that was basically just motivational speeches overlaid on ambient noise," said sophomore Emma Liu. "I removed it and replaced it with K-pop. My GPA went up. I don't understand it, but I won't question it."

The industry adapts

Independent artists are already pivoting. Several bedroom producers announced new albums specifically designed to distract students from their studies while simultaneously helping them complete those studies. One track, "Nothing About Finals," debuted at number one on Billboard's Lo-Fi charts within hours.

For now, researchers recommend students keep an eye out for lyrical content when building focus playlists. Songs mentioning coffee shops, sunrise, and emotional growth appear safe. Any song containing the phrase "deadline approaching" should be avoided entirely.

"The message is clear," Dr. Martinez concluded. "If you want to study effectively, listen to music that is completely irrelevant to what you're studying. Preferably about something joyful. Or dancing. Just not studying."
