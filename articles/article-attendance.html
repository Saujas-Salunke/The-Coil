Student Attended College Every Day for a Month. What Happened Next Shocked His Entire Class

One student's bold experiment in perfect attendance reveals the surprising limits of showing up to class.

By Renata Dubois Published September 20, 2026 Updated September 20, 2026 Reading time 3 min

The Coil is a fictional parody publication. All stories and quotes are created for demonstration purposes.

A college student recently attempted something almost unheard of: attending every lecture for an entire month. The experiment began normally. He arrived on time, attended practicals and listened to lectures.

By the second week, his classmates had started asking questions. "Are you okay?" By the third week, people became suspicious. "Why are you actually here?"

The shocking revelation

By the end of the month, the student had discovered something remarkable. He knew what was being taught. Unfortunately, he also discovered that attending every lecture did not automatically make the assignments disappear.

"I thought attendance would solve everything. Turns out, knowing what the professor said doesn't mean you're prepared to write a 3,000-word essay about it," said the student, now recovering from exhaustion in his dorm room.

Faculty members noticed the anomaly. One professor reported being genuinely surprised when a student showed consistent engagement throughout term time. "We prepared attendance alerts in case someone missed classes," explained Professor Anjali Nair. "We didn't prepare for someone attending every single one."

Campus reacts

The broader student population responded with equal parts admiration and concern. Group chats dedicated discussions about whether this behavior was sustainable, admirable, or both.

"I asked him if he was sick. Then I realized he wasn't sick—he was just... there. Every day," said one second-year student who requested anonymity.

The lesson learned

The experiment concluded with a nuanced understanding of educational systems. Perfect attendance can improve course completion rates, but it does not alter fundamental truths about coursework volume, deadline pressure, or the universal desire to skip 8 AM lectures.

For now, the student has returned to a more traditional attendance pattern, averaging approximately 73 percent presence. "It turns out 73 percent is the sweet spot," he explained. "You get credit, but you preserve your sanity."

Attendance can increase your attendance percentage, but it cannot increase your willingness to attend an 8 AM lecture.
