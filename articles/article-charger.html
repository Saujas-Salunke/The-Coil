This Man Charged His Phone to 100% Every Night. What Happened Next Shocked Him

One man's month-long experiment in optimal phone charging reveals the surprising truth about battery care myths.

By Renata Dubois Published September 20, 2026 Updated September 20, 2026 Reading time 3 min

The Coil is a fictional parody publication. All stories and quotes are created for demonstration purposes.

For an entire month, Arjun charged his smartphone to 100% before going to bed. He expected something dramatic to happen. It didn't.

The phone reached 100%, displayed a small charging icon and remained completely indifferent to his experiment. The following morning, he had a fully charged phone.

A remarkable breakthrough

After 30 days, he noticed something surprising. His battery was no longer at 12% when he left home.

"I thought maybe the lithium-ion cells would explode. Or develop consciousness. Something. Instead, my phone just... worked," said Arjun, reflecting on his journey of self-discovery.

Tech experts we reached for comment expressed mild surprise at the findings. "Charging a device to its full capacity typically results in that device having full capacity available for use," explained Dr. Sameer Kapoor, a mobile technology specialist who agreed to speak on condition of anonymity.

"It's exactly what we would expect to happen when someone charges their phone," Kapoor continued. "But I suppose people are trying many unconventional methods these days. Plugging in your phone at night is surprisingly underrated."

The broader implications

Other potential experiments Arjun considered include using his phone, turning it on after pressing the power button and attempting to send text messages after typing words on the keyboard.

Industry analysts say this type of user behavior—actually using devices as intended—is becoming increasingly rare. Many consumers now prefer elaborate rituals like removing protective cases before charging or whispering encouraging words to their devices.

Phase two begins

Arjun has now begun another experiment: remembering to bring his charger. Early results suggest this practice may prevent situations where a phone has battery but cannot be recharged away from home.

"Day one went well," Arjun reported. "I brought my charger and used it once. Revolutionary." Further testing is underway to determine whether remembering charging cables constitutes a sustainable lifestyle change.

For readers inspired to try similar experiments, experts recommend starting with the basics. Charging your phone works. Using it when it's charged also works. These discoveries, while mundane, can fundamentally transform your relationship with technology.
