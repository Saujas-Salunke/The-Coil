College Student Claims He Has "Started Studying Early" — Investigation Ongoing

The claim, made Sunday evening, has yet to be corroborated by any physical evidence of a textbook being open.

By Tomás Reyes Published September 15, 2026 Updated September 15, 2026 Reading time 5 min

The Coil is a fictional parody publication. All stories and quotes are created for demonstration purposes.

A junior majoring in economics announced Sunday evening, via a group chat message timestamped 8:47 p.m., that he had "started studying early" for a midterm scheduled eleven days from now. The claim, which was met with a mixture of skepticism and cautious optimism from friends, remains under informal investigation as of press time.

"He said he 'started,'" said roommate Beatriz Alonso, "but when I walked past his room twenty minutes later, he was watching a video about a video game he doesn't even play. I'm not accusing him of anything. I'm just saying the studying hasn't been independently observed."

A pattern of claims

This is not the first time the student, who asked to be referred to only as "T." for this story, has made an early-studying claim. Records reviewed by The Coil show similar statements made at the beginning of at least three previous semesters, none of which were followed by any confirmed studying prior to the 48-hour mark before the actual exam.

"I have started. Starting is a state of mind," T. said when reached for comment, seated at his desk with three tabs open, none of which were related to economics.

T. maintains that the studying has, in fact, begun, describing it as being in "the planning phase," which he defines as having opened his laptop, considered opening a document, and then closing the laptop to think about opening it again later.

What the evidence shows

A brief review of T.'s recent activity shows one (1) highlighted paragraph in a course PDF, believed to have occurred accidentally while scrolling. No notes have been taken. No practice problems have been attempted. A sticky note reading "START TODAY" has been present on his desk for six days and has not moved.

Friends remain hopeful

Despite the lack of visible progress, friends say they remain cautiously optimistic. "He does eventually study," said classmate Wren Okafor. "It just tends to happen in a single, terrifying 14-hour session two days before the exam. I've seen it. It's not sustainable, but it is, in some strange way, impressive."

T. has since updated the group chat to clarify that he has "basically started," a claim that, as of publication, remains similarly unverified.
