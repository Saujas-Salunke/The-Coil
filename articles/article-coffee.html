This Student Bought A $7 Coffee And Somehow Turned It Into A Personality

Friends confirm the purchase has now been mentioned in at least four unrelated conversations, none of which were about coffee.

By Marcus Oduya Published September 17, 2026 Updated September 17, 2026 Reading time 5 min

The Coil is a fictional parody publication. All stories and quotes are created for demonstration purposes.

It started, as these things often do, with a receipt. A sophomore, who we are identifying only as "J." at their request, purchased a oat-milk vanilla latte from the campus coffee cart on Tuesday morning for a reported $7.15. By Tuesday evening, the purchase had become, according to three separate witnesses, "kind of all they talk about now."

"It's not that the coffee was bad," said roommate Dana Kessler. "It's that it was apparently the best thing that's ever happened to them. I've heard about the foam-to-milk ratio more times than I've heard about their actual major."

A purchase, elevated

What began as a simple beverage has, in the space of a week, become a full identity marker. J. has reportedly referred to themselves as "more of a latte person now," has recommended the coffee cart to no fewer than nine classmates, and was seen taking a photo of the cup from a "moodier angle" near a window.

"I didn't know coffee could be a personality trait until this week. Turns out it can. Turns out it very much can," said classmate Owen Price.

Baristas at the cart, who declined to be named for fear of encouraging further visits, confirmed that the latte in question is, in fact, a standard menu item that has existed since the cart opened two years ago. "We didn't do anything special," one said. "We just made the coffee. That's the whole story."

The ripple effects

Since the purchase, J. has reportedly brought up the coffee during a group project meeting, a study session, and — according to one unconfirmed account — a phone call with their mother that was ostensibly about something else entirely. Group chat records reviewed by The Coil show a noticeable uptick in coffee-related messages beginning almost immediately after the purchase.

Is this normal?

Campus culture researchers, when reached for comment, described the phenomenon as "extremely normal, if slightly exhausting." One noted that beverage-based identity shifts are common in the first month of a semester, when students are especially open to redefining themselves through small, low-stakes purchases.

"It's low commitment," the researcher explained. "You can't dye your hair every week, but you can buy a latte. The latte becomes the whole vibe."

As of publication, J. has purchased the same latte four more times and has begun referring to the coffee cart as "my place." The cart, for its part, remains a coffee cart.
